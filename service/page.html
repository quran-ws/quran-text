Positioning

Why here, and not elsewhere.

Four things no other Qurʾān source gives a developer at once.

Seven riwāyāt, not one.

Ḥafṣ, Shuʿbah, Warsh, Qālūn, al-Dūrī, al-Sūsī and al-Bazzī — each its own printed muṣḥaf, with its own spelling, its own āyah count and its own KFGQPC face. Most sources publish Ḥafṣ and stop there.

KFGQPC bytes, not our edition.

Nothing here is re-typed, corrected or normalised. The file you download is cut from the King Fahd Complex release and carries that release's sha256 in its own header, so you can prove it without asking us.

One URL. No key, no sign-up.

Same URL, same bytes, forever. No account to create, no rate limit to hit, no version that breaks under you — the URL is the file.

Word numbers shared across all seven.

Word 5,177 is إِلَٰهَ in every riwāyah, even where the āyah numbers diverge. That is the join key for translations, grammar and audio timings across readings.

The fonts

One font per riwāyah.
Ship it with the text.

Each text uses letters and signs that only its own KFGQPC font draws: the open tanwīn, the small alifs, the pause marks. In any other Arabic font some of them show as gaps or boxes. The fonts are KFGQPC's own, redistributed with the text.

Provenance

Nothing here is unsourced.

Every file is cut from a King Fahd Complex release and carries that release's name and sha256 in its own header. If a byte moved, you can prove it without asking us.

Hashed at the source

Every download carries an X-Checksum-SHA256 header, and the provenance header names the KFGQPC package it was cut from.

Reproducible build

One script turns the KFGQPC releases into every file on this page. Run it yourself and compare hashes.

Boundaries, cited

Āyah correspondences come from quranpedia/qiraat-ayah-map (MIT), not our own judgement.

Terms you inherit

The text is KFGQPC's. Redistribution is subject to their terms — read them before you ship.
